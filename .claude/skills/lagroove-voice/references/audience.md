# Who Lagroove Talks To

Write to **her** — picture one person, not a demographic block.

## The core audience

**Young women in Boulder, Colorado, with a heavy CU-Boulder student skew.**
She's roughly 19–32, into wellness and aesthetics, and lives the "clean green
girl" lifestyle: matcha runs, sage-green activewear sets, gold jewelry, tote
bags, Pilates-princess energy, a curated Instagram. She wants to feel strong,
toned, and good in her body — and she wants the *vibe* to be cute while she does
it. Community matters to her; she'd come with friends and wants a "second home."

Secondary / also-welcome (don't center, but never exclude):
- **Beginners & the Lagree-curious** — intimidated by hardcore gyms; needs
  reassurance and a low-pressure entry point.
- **Experienced Lagree/Pilates/boutique-fitness** members refining their form.
- **Expecting & new moms** — core stability, low-impact, joint-friendly.
- **Older / "feel-good enthusiasts" & athletes** — "designed for all" really means it.

## What she responds to

- **Aesthetics first.** The green, the disco ball, the groovy type — the look is
  the hook. Copy rides on top of a beautiful image.
- **Belonging & community** over performance metrics. "Find your people," not
  "hit your PR."
- **Body positivity & self-care framing.** Affirmations, "do it for your future
  self," progress-not-perfection. She's opting *in* to feeling good, not punishing
  herself.
- **Low-commitment invitations.** "2 weeks, no commitment," "first class on us,"
  "come see what the hype's about." She's price-aware (student) and wary of
  lock-in.
- **Social proof & FOMO, gently.** "Boulder's grooviest," class selling out, the
  Disco Ball Draw — she wants to be where the fun is.
- **Convenience cues.** Boulder local, easy parking, on the app, fits a student
  schedule.

## How to write to her

- Second person, warm, friend-to-friend. "You" and "let's."
- Cute and playful beats clever-corporate. Puns welcome; jargon isn't.
- Reassure the beginner in the same breath you hype the regular.
- Reference real Boulder/CU + wellness culture lightly (matcha, the disco ball,
  sage sets) — she should feel seen, not pandered to.
- Keep money talk light and low-pressure; lead with how it'll *feel*, then the offer.

## What to avoid

- Hardcore "no pain no gain" gym-bro energy — wrong audience entirely.
- Weight-loss-shame or "earn your body" framing.
- Corporate wellness-speak ("optimize your fitness journey").
- Assuming she's an expert — but also never talking down to her.
