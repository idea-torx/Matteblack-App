# Trove — 15s animated ad

Model: MiniMax H3 Max. Prompt Expansion: **Disabled**. Generation ~16.6s per run, so reroll freely.

Paste everything inside the block below as a single prompt.

```
Create a 15-second 2D hand-drawn animated advertisement in a warm storybook style, flat cel animation with visible pencil and crayon texture, gentle paper grain, no 3D, no photographic footage, no live action, no camera lens effects.

The entire voice-over is spoken in Australian-accented English, the everyday accent of Sydney or Melbourne. This applies to every line without exception.

Pace. This is unhurried and warm, the opposite of a hard-cutting tech ad. Movement is continuous and springy rather than snappy: things bounce, settle, and breathe. Cuts are soft and infrequent, roughly one every three seconds, and each new scene grows out of the yarn thread already on screen rather than replacing it. Nothing strobes, nothing flickers, nothing whip-pans.

Art direction. The ground of the film is warm cream paper #faf8f2 with a faint drawn texture, and above it a soft daytime sky ramp running from #8bbaee at the top through #dcebf9 and #E9E2EF to #FBF2E4 at the horizon. Two layers of low rolling hills sit along the bottom of every outdoor scene, drawn in soft green with a slightly wobbling hand-inked contour and small grass tufts. Ink is charcoal #1a1a1a, always soft-edged, never pure black fills. The accent palette is strictly amber #fdd554, warm gold #ffc25e, sky blue #94d2fa, leaf green #add473, tan #d4a373 and a single rose #d473bc used only once, on one handwritten word. Colours are flat with occasional crayon shading inside the outline, slightly overshooting the line the way a child's colouring does. Everything drawn has a hand-inked, slightly uneven contour with a faint pencil under-sketch showing through. Round corners, soft shapes, no hard geometry, no rectangles with sharp corners, no grids, no charts, no dashboards, no UI chrome, no phone bezels, no cursors.

Characters. All characters are yarn tots: small round creatures made of a single wound ball of coloured yarn with visible strand texture, stubby yarn limbs, simple dot eyes, no mouths, no faces beyond the eyes, no human features, no hair, no clothing. They are palm-sized, they hop rather than walk, and they squash and stretch as they move. There are four of them in the film and their yarn colours are amber, sky blue, leaf green and tan. They never speak and their eyes never become expressive faces.

Forbidden throughout: photorealism, 3D render, CGI shading, glossy plastic surfaces, lens flare, bokeh, depth of field, film grain, glitch, neon, dark mode, near-black backgrounds, sharp-cornered rectangles, charts, graphs, progress bars, dashboards, phone or laptop screens, keyboards, cursors, brains, lightbulbs, gears, human faces, hands, uppercase type.

Signature motif, the thread. One continuous amber yarn thread, colour #fdd554, is present in every single passage without exception. It is never cut, never duplicated, never recoloured. It only changes role: first a loose end trailing from a tangled knot, then a path the tots follow across the hills, then the line that writes a word, then the stitching that binds a small book, then the outline of the final shape. Its motion is always a smooth travelling draw-on, drawn by an unseen hand, one continuous stroke.

Typography. Use a warm geometric sans with generous round counters, semibold, tight leading, soft charcoal #1a1a1a on cream, and one word per film set instead in a loose handwritten marker script. Every phrase is set in sentence case — an initial capital on the first word only, every other letter lowercase. Do not render any phrase in uppercase, all caps or small caps. Type is always sharp and still, never blurred, extruded, outlined or shadowed, and it never rotates or bends.

Text handling. Render every phrase as one complete, professionally typeset, precomposed layer that fades up whole over about four frames and holds. Never construct, scramble, morph, glitch or animate individual letters, with one exception named below. The only phrases that appear in the entire film are exactly these four, in this order and in this exact casing: "You've done this before." then "Play a five-minute story." then "The kind liar." then "A record that remembers you." No other words, letters, numbers, logos, wordmarks or symbols appear anywhere at any time. Anywhere writing would normally sit — on the pages of the little book, on the small cards the tots hold, on the signposts in the hills — leave the surface blank or use soft featureless pencil squiggles instead of letters. The single exception is the phrase "The kind liar.", where the last word only is drawn in rose #d473bc handwritten marker script by the amber thread travelling as a pen, completing in about half a second; the two words before it are typeset and already in place.

Passage one, zero to three seconds, the knot. Camera slowly pushes in. On cream paper, a dense messy ball of tangled multicoloured yarn sits alone, rocking slightly as if something is stuck inside it. One amber thread end works its way free and flicks upward. The knot loosens and unwinds in one smooth motion into an amber yarn tot, who lands with a small bounce and blinks. The phrase "You've done this before." fades up beneath it in soft charcoal.

Passage two, three to six point five seconds, the walk. Camera pans gently left to right. The amber thread stretches out ahead of the tot and lays itself down as a winding path across soft green rolling hills under the sky ramp, with small grass tufts and two slow drifting cream clouds. The amber tot hops along the path and three more yarn tots — sky blue, leaf green, tan — bounce in from the side and fall in behind, all four moving in a loose springy line. Along the path the thread loops upward twice into simple round arch shapes the tots hop through, each arch settling with a small wobble. The phrase "Play a five-minute story." fades up in the sky above the hills.

Passage three, six point five to ten seconds, the choice. Camera holds still. The path arrives at a wide flat stretch of hilltop. The thread loops down and draws three soft-cornered torn-paper cards standing side by side, cream with slightly ragged edges, each blank apart from a faint pencil squiggle. The amber tot hesitates in front of them, leaning toward one card, then another, then hops decisively onto the third; the two unchosen cards tip over backwards and dissolve into loose yarn fibres that the thread reels back in. The chosen card lifts and the phrase "The kind liar." appears on the cream ground in front of it, the first two words typeset and still, the final word drawn live in rose handwritten marker by the amber thread travelling as a pen tip.

Passage four, ten to thirteen seconds, the record. Camera pulls back slowly. The card lowers itself flat and more cards of the same size flock in from off screen and stack softly on top of it, one every few frames, until they form a small book. The amber thread stitches the spine in three quick loops and the book falls open, its pages blank cream with faint pencil squiggle lines and small soft-cornered colour patches in amber, sky blue, leaf green and tan appearing down the margins, each landing with a tiny bounce. The four yarn tots sit in a row along the top edge of the open book, legs swinging. The phrase "A record that remembers you." fades up above them.

Passage five, thirteen to fifteen seconds, the pill, then hold. Camera locked, no movement. The tots and the book settle downward out of frame and the amber thread lifts free, travels across the empty cream ground and draws itself into one horizontal fully rounded pill shape, centred, occupying about half the frame width, which fills with warm amber #ffc25e and holds a soft warm inner glow. The pill is never black and never dark. Nothing is written on it. All movement stops completely and the final frame is a still, sharp cream frame with the single warm amber rounded pill centred in it, unchanged through to the very end of the clip.

Transitions. Every transition is carried by the amber thread: it draws the next scene on, or reels the previous one in as loose fibre. There are no dissolves, no wipes, no flashes, no cuts to a blank frame. The thread is never absent from screen.

Voice-over, a single Australian voice, male or female, speaking Australian-accented English, the everyday accent of Sydney or Melbourne, throughout, never American, never British, never a neutral or transatlantic accent, and the accent never drifts or changes between lines, warm, dry and quirky, quietly amused, unhurried, close-mic, no reverb, mixed clearly above the music. Speech begins immediately at the very start of the clip, on the first frame, with no silent opening and no instrumental introduction, and is spread evenly across the entire fifteen seconds, never crammed into the second half and never rushed. The gaps between lines are short and even. Lines: from zero to two point eight seconds, "Every quiz gave you four letters and forgot you by Friday." From three point two to six seconds, "Trove gives you a five-minute story to play instead." From six point four to nine point four seconds, "It watches what you actually chose, and names it." From nine point eight to twelve point four seconds, "Every story you play adds a page." From twelve point eight to fourteen point six seconds, "Trove. A record that remembers you."

Music, playful and quirky but mixed low, always under the voice and never competing with it: a bouncy pizzicato string figure with soft marimba, a curious little whistled countermelody and light brushed hand percussion, tempo springy, dynamics kept gentle throughout. The tune is mischievous rather than grand. It picks up its bounce as the tots start walking at three point two seconds, hesitates for a beat with the tot at the cards around seven point five seconds, and lands on one soft cheerful resolving chord as the pill forms at thirteen point two seconds. No drums kit, no big build, no epic swell.

Sound design, soft and tactile, sitting on the animation rather than on cuts. A dry fibrous unravelling sound as the knot comes loose. A small rounded boing on every tot bounce, each a slightly different pitch. A light paper rustle for each card that appears, tips over or stacks. A soft felt-tip squeak as the handwritten word is drawn. Three quick thread-pull sounds for the stitched spine. One warm low chime as the pill fills, then quiet room tone under the final hold.

Negative prompt: uppercase, all caps, capital letters, small caps, extra text, gibberish text, warping letters, morphing type, logo, wordmark, brand name, numbers, symbols, human faces, hands, people, mouths, expressive cartoon faces, 3D, CGI, photorealism, plastic sheen, lens flare, bokeh, depth of field, film grain, glitch, strobing, neon, dark background, near-black, black button, sharp-cornered rectangles, phone screen, laptop, keyboard, cursor, UI panels, charts, graphs, progress bars, dashboards, brain, lightbulb, gears, fast cutting, whip pan, camera shake, motion at the end of the clip, American accent, British accent, neutral accent, transatlantic accent, accent drift, multiple voices, silent opening, instrumental intro, delayed voice-over, rushed speech, voice-over crammed into the second half.
```

## Notes for future edits

- **Motion spine** is the single amber thread `#fdd554`. It must be on screen in every passage or the transitions stop reading as one continuous drawing.
- **Palette** is the repo's own: cream `#faf8f2`, ink `#1a1a1a`, sky ramp `#8bbaee → #dcebf9 → #E9E2EF → #FBF2E4` (`house.css:139`), accents `#fdd554` `#ffc25e` `#94d2fa` `#add473` `#d4a373`. Rose `#d473bc` is deliberately rationed to one handwritten word — Helen's note is that pink isn't really a Trove colour.
- **Casing**: phrases are written in sentence case *in the prompt itself*; the model mirrors whatever casing it is given.
- **The one animated word** is the exception to "never animate letters". If takes come back with scrambled or morphing type elsewhere, delete the exception paragraph and set "The kind liar." fully typeset.
- **VO timing**: takes tend to hold the voice back and then cram it late, so the prompt says explicitly that speech starts on the first frame and spreads evenly. Keep that sentence and keep line one short.
- **Voice** must be Australian — that is the one invariant, and it is stated twice on purpose: once in the opening paragraph (models weight the top of the prompt heavily and ignore an accent named only at the bottom) and again in the voice-over paragraph. If a take still comes back non-Australian, reroll; do not soften either mention.
- **Voice gender** gender is free and quirky is welcome. **Music** is playful and quirky but deliberately mixed low — if a take comes back with the bed over the VO, reroll rather than fixing it in the edit.
- **Ending is the amber pill, never a black button** — same rule as the static boards. No wordmark on screen; the name is carried by the VO.
- **Product story** maps to the passages: tangle (play) → choice (torn-note options) → Facet (the named trait) → Living Record (the book). If a passage has to be cut for time, cut passage two, not three.
- Generate 10–15 takes and assemble the best passages; trim ~1s of motion ramp off any inserted clip.
